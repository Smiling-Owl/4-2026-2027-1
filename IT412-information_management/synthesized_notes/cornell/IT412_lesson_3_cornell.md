# Knowledge Area 1: Information Technology

**Date:** 2026-09-09

| Cue / Keywords | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Information Technology (IT)** | Refers to the specific technical components: hardware, software, and communications. IT components alone may have no useful function and must be engineered into usable systems that serve organizational needs. |
| **The Nature of Technology** | - Traditional IT categories are becoming less relevant as devices like smartphones integrate thousands of components into a single unit.<br>- More useful to focus on enduring trends and patterns of innovation & obsolescence rather than specific, rapidly changing technologies.<br>- IT is now highly commoditized and pre-packaged, allowing easy, almost effortless purchase and integration from a catalogue.<br>- The real, time-consuming effort lies in loading the specific content and essential details to make a system functional for a business (significant human endeavor). |
| **Managerial Issues in IT** | - **Technology management scope:** Outsourced technology requires senior management to weigh risks & benefits for investments in hardware, software, and trained personnel.<br>- **Managing suppliers:** Cultivate the most useful supplier relationship type; being a good customer sometimes outweighs aggressive supplier treatment.<br>- **Acquiring technology:** Careful justification and tendering to avoid excessive "just in case" spending.<br>- **Managing the technology portfolio:** Align current/future tech investments with business strategy and track owned technology and its physical location.<br>- **Technology competencies:** Maintain internal competencies to track developments, understand IT's nature and origins, and foster experimentation, innovation, and evaluation.<br>- **Budget management:** Support both centralized capital budgets and local departmental funding. |
| **IT Architecture vs. Infrastructure** | - **IT Architecture:** Blueprint for translating business strategy into a system plan; defines *what needs to be built*.<br>- **IT Infrastructure:** Everything supporting the flow & processing of information (hardware, software, data, network components that are chosen and assembled); defines *what is built*. |
| **Business Strategy → IT Infrastructure** | - **Strategy:** Business goals and vision.<br>- **Architecture:** Blueprint and plans.<br>- **Infrastructure:** Actual hardware, software, network, data. |
| **The Manager's Role** | - Communicate business vision to IT architects.<br>- Understand what to expect from architecture and infrastructure.<br>- Ensure plans don't block important business considerations.<br>- Be a knowledgeable participant in discussions. |
| **Strategy → Architecture → Infrastructure** | - Strategy defines the overarching direction; goals become measurable; business requirements state what the system must accomplish; architectural requirements identify data, process, and security needs — culminating in the architecture blueprint.<br>- **Functional specifications** describe what each component must do; **technical specifications** detail how to implement; components are combined into a coherent, viable infrastructure. |
| **Analysis Framework (Components)** | - **Hardware:** Physical components for computation, storage, transmission.<br>- **Software:** Programs enabling work (system software + applications).<br>- **Network:** Hardware/software for local/long-distance networking.<br>- **Data:** Electronic representation of numbers and text. Each component is analyzed via What? / Who? / Where? questions. |
| **Common IT Architectures** | - **Centralized:** One central computer system runs all applications & stores all data; all functionality in one place for easier management.<br>- **Decentralized:** Computing power spread among devices in different locations; addresses modularization & scalability.<br>- **Service-Oriented (Cloud-based):** Software broken into services, orchestrated & connected for agility, reusability, and rapid app design.<br>- **Software-Defined:** Infrastructure reconfigured autonomously based on rules to flexibly manage resources. |
| **Enterprise Architecture (EA)** | - Comprehensive framework for creating the blueprint for all information systems and their interrelationships in the firm.<br>- Based on business strategy; specifies how IT supports business processes, aligns with business needs, and produces business outcomes.<br>- Includes standard technical capabilities/activities and guidelines for making choices.<br>- **Layers:** Business, Application, Information, Technology. |

### Summary
Information Technology (IT) refers to the specific technical components — hardware, software, and communications — that must be engineered into usable systems to serve organizational needs. Because technology is increasingly commoditized, the real value lies in loading the specific content that makes systems functional. Managers must navigate several issues: technology management scope, supplier relationships, acquisition, portfolio alignment, internal competencies, and budgeting. The critical distinction is between IT Architecture (the blueprint defining what needs to be built) and IT Infrastructure (the chosen and assembled components defining what is actually built). Strategy drives architecture, which drives infrastructure, guided by a framework analyzing hardware, software, network, and data. Common architectures include centralized, decentralized, service-oriented (cloud), and software-defined, culminating in Enterprise Architecture — a strategy-based blueprint for all information systems.
