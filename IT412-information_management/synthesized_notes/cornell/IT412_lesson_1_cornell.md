# Introduction to Information Management

**Date:** 2026-08-10

| Cue / Keywords | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **DIKW Hierarchy** | - **Data:** Raw, unprocessed facts without context (e.g., number 42).<br>- **Information:** Processed, structured data with context (e.g., 42 computers offline).<br>- **Knowledge:** Application of experience and insight to understand "how" and "why" (e.g., computers offline due to patch issue).<br>- **Wisdom:** Application of knowledge for sound judgments and predictions (e.g., roll back update, schedule future patches). |
| **Information Management (IM)** | - Systematic discipline of collecting, storing, managing, securing, distributing, and archiving/destroying information.<br>- Treats information as a valuable corporate asset. |
| **Key Concepts in IM** | - **Information Lifecycle Management (ILM):** Managing information from creation to destruction.<br>- **Data Quality & Integrity:** Ensuring accurate, consistent, and reliable data.<br>- **Information Security & Privacy:** Protecting from unauthorized access and ensuring compliance.<br>- **Accessibility & Usability:** Easily retrievable by authorized personnel. |
| **Goal and Value of IM** | - **Primary Objective:** Deliver the *right information* to the *right person* at the *exact right time* in the *right format*.<br>- **Benefits:** Organizational efficiency (reduces silos), strategic decision-making (data-driven insights), risk management (protects assets). |
| **IM vs. KM** | - **Information Management:** Focuses on explicit, tangible assets (mechanics of handling data).<br>- **Knowledge Management (KM):** Focuses on intangible assets (human expertise, sharing culture). |

---

### Summary
Information Management (IM) is the discipline of treating information as a valuable corporate asset throughout its lifecycle (ILM). Understanding the DIKW hierarchy (Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom) is essential for transforming raw facts into actionable insights. While IM focuses on the mechanics of tangible data, Knowledge Management focuses on intangible human expertise. Effective IM ensures data quality, security, and accessibility, ultimately driving organizational efficiency, risk management, and strategic decision-making by delivering the right information at the right time.
