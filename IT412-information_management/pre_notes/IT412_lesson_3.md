# Knowledge Area 1: Information Technology

## 1. What is Information Technology?
Information technology (IT) refers to the specific technical components: hardware, software, and communications. IT components alone may have no useful function and must be engineered into usable systems that serve organizational needs.

## 2. The Nature of Technology
* The traditional categories of IT are becoming less relevant as devices like smartphones integrate thousands of components into a single unit.
* It is more useful to focus on enduring trends and patterns of innovation and obsolescence rather than specific, rapidly changing technologies.
* Some experts argue that IT is now highly commoditized and pre-packaged, allowing for easy, almost effortless purchase and integration from a catalogue.
* The real, time-consuming effort now lies in loading the specific content and essential details required to make a system functional for a business, which requires significant human endeavor.

## 3. Managerial Issues in IT
* **Technology management scope:** Increasingly outsourced technology requires senior management to weigh risks and benefits when determining investments in hardware, software, and trained personnel.
* **Managing suppliers:** Organizations must identify and cultivate the most useful relationship type with technology suppliers, recognize different relationship kinds, and manage costs. In most cases, being a good customer sometimes outweighs aggressive supplier treatment.
* **Acquiring technology:** Effective acquisition management requires careful justification and tendering procedures to avoid excessive "just in case" spending, alongside clear criteria for selecting appropriate supply arrangements.
* **Managing the technology portfolio:** A portfolio perspective aligns current and future technology investments with business strategies, ensuring awareness of owned technology and its physical location.
* **Technology competencies:** Even with heavy supplier reliance, every organization must maintain internal competencies to track developments, understand IT's nature and origins, and foster experimentation, innovation, and evaluation.
* **Budget management:** A single budget is rarely optimal for acquiring new technology. Policies should support both centralized capital budgets and local departmental funding, with clear understanding and application of these approaches.

## 4. IT Architecture vs. IT Infrastructure
* **IT Architecture:** The blueprint for translating business strategy into a system plan. Defines what needs to be built.
* **IT Infrastructure:** Everything that supports the flow and processing of information. Includes hardware, software, data, and network components that are chosen and assembled. Defines what is built.

## 5. The Business Strategy to IT Infrastructure Process
* **Strategy:** Business goals and vision.
* **Architecture:** Blueprint and plans.
* **Infrastructure:** Actual hardware, software, network, data.

## 6. The Manager's Role
* Communicate business vision to IT architects.
* Understand what to expect from architecture and infrastructure.
* Ensure plans don't block important business considerations.
* Be a knowledgeable participant in discussions.

## 7. From Business Strategy to IT Architecture
* The strategy defines the overarching direction for a business.
* The specific goals develop measurable goals in line with the strategy.
* The business requirements list down what the system must accomplish.
* The architectural requirements identify the data, process, and security needs.
* All culminate into the architecture, which is the designed blueprint.

## 8. From IT Architecture to Infrastructure
* The functional specifications describe what each component must do.
* The technical specifications detail how to implement the system.
* The components must be combined in a coherent structure to create a viable infrastructure.

## 9. Framework for Infrastructure and Architecture Analysis
When developing a framework for transforming business strategy into architecture and then into infrastructure, these basic components should be considered:
* **Hardware:** Physical components for computation, storage, transmission.
* **Software:** Programs enabling work (system software + applications).
* **Network:** Hardware/software for local/long-distance networking.
* **Data:** Electronic representation of numbers and text.

Example questions to ask for each basic component:

| Component | What? | Who? | Where? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware | What type of devices will users use? | Who knows most about servers? | Centralized or distributed? |
| Software | Does strategy require ERP software? | Who is affected by move to SAP? | Multiple database instances? |
| Network | How should network be structured? | Who needs network connection? | Lease cable or use satellite? |
| Data | What data needed for sales system? | Who needs access to sensitive data? | Cloud or data center? |

## 10. Common IT Architectures
* **Centralized Architecture:** A large central computer system runs all applications and stores all data. All functionality is located in one place to make it easier to manage.
* **Decentralized Architecture:** Computing power is spread out among a number of devices in different locations. Typically chosen to modularize and address concerns about scalability.
* **Service-Oriented (Cloud-based) Architecture:** Software is broken down into services, orchestrated and connected to each other in order to be agile, reusable, and facilitate rapid app design.
* **Software-Defined Architecture:** Infrastructure can be reconfigured autonomously based on rules to flexibly manage resources.

## 11. Enterprise Architecture
* A comprehensive framework for creating the blueprint for all information systems and their interrelationships in the business firm.
* The basis of enterprise architecture is business strategy.
* Specifies how information technologies support business processes, align with business needs, and produce business outcomes.
* Includes the standard technical capabilities and activities for all parts of the enterprise and guidelines for making choices.

### Enterprise Architecture Layers
* **Business Layer:** The common business organizations, strategies, functions, and models that describe how business is done and the important cross-functional business processes used in the business.
* **Application Layer:** The applications that support business processes, specifying relationships between processes and between applications.
* **Information Layer:** The enterprise information assets used by business processes and enterprise applications. Specifies physical storage and access for data such as operational and analytics data.
* **Technology Layer:** The hardware and software infrastructure, including IT standards, structures, and the plans for IT, which create the technical foundation for the organization.
