# Systems Integration: Core Concepts and Fundamentals

## 1. The "What" and "Why" of System Integration
System integration is the process of putting or bringing together the different systems into one system[cite: 3]. It ensures that these interdependent systems function together as a unified system[cite: 3]. Furthermore, it facilitates communication across the network[cite: 3]. When done poorly or in an ad-hoc manner, this is sometimes referred to as "Spaghetti Integration"[cite: 3].

### Enterprise Integration
Enterprise Integration goes a step further than basic system integration. It is the strategic consideration of the process, methods, tools and technologies associated with achieving interoperability between IT applications[cite: 3]. This interoperability occurs both within and external to the enterprise to enable collaborative business processes[cite: 3].

Key characteristics of Enterprise Integration include:
* It is business driven rather than technology driven[cite: 3].
* It coordinates business processes with and cross different parts of the enterprise[cite: 3].
* It involves multiple stakeholders[cite: 3].
* It adopts a strategic rather than tactical or localized view[cite: 3].

---

## 2. Common Business Problems Addressed
Organizations pursue integration to solve specific operational pain points:
* **Information Aggregation:** Presenting information from multiple IT sources into a single view[cite: 3].
* **Single point of data entry:** Replacing the need for manual and duplicate data entry into multiple IT applications[cite: 3].
* **Process inefficiency:** Reducing effort and time for business processes to complete[cite: 3].
* **Web channel integration:** Enabling web-based customers and other stakeholders access[cite: 3].
* **Supplier integration and supply chain optimization:** Enabling a supplier to integrate with a larger company’s business process or an electronic marketplace[cite: 3].

---

## 3. Systems Integration Methods
Architects use different methods to physically and logically connect systems:
* **Horizontal Integration:** Creation of a subsystem meant to be the interface between all other subsystems[cite: 3]. This is also referred to as an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB)[cite: 3].
* **Vertical Integration:** Creation of depositories or silos[cite: 3].
* **Star Integration:** Sub-systems are connected with other sub-systems using point-to-point connections[cite: 3].
* **Common data format/integration:** Setting a common or application-independent format, or providing a service that does the transformation to or from one application into the common application[cite: 3].

---

## 4. Integration Types
The primary categories of integration include:
* **Enterprise Application Integration (EAI):**[cite: 3] Connecting internal workflows and applications to trigger real-time actions.
* **Data Integration (DI):**[cite: 3] Consolidating raw data from various databases into a single repository for analytics and reporting. 
* **Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) Integration:**[cite: 3] Secure, computer-to-computer exchange of standardized business documents between external partner companies.

---

## 5. General Steps in Systems Integration
Executing a successful integration project generally follows these phases:
1. Requirements Gathering[cite: 3].
2. Data Analysis[cite: 3].
3. Design Architecture[cite: 3].
4. Design Systems integration[cite: 3].
5. System Implementation[cite: 3].

---

## 6. Assessing the Need for Integration
To determine if an organization requires systems integration, ask the following diagnostic questions:
* Is there a requirement to collate information from several IT systems in real time to meet specific business objectives?[cite: 3]
* Does the organisation have many IT systems that need to communicate with each other?[cite: 3]
* Is there a need to key in and update the same details in separate IT systems?[cite: 3]
* Is it difficult and time consuming to extract information from several IT systems?[cite: 3]
* Does the organisation batch upload information from one IT system to another?[cite: 3]
* Is the same information duplicated across many IT systems?[cite: 3]
* Is there an issue with data inconsistency between IT systems?[cite: 3]
