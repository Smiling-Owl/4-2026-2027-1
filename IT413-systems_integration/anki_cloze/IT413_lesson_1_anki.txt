{{c1::System Integration}} is the process of bringing {{c2::different systems together into one unified, interoperable system}} that facilitates {{c3::communication across the network}}.
The process of bringing {{c1::different systems together into one unified, interoperable system}} is known as {{c2::System Integration}}.
Poor or ad-hoc system integration is referred to as {{c1::Spaghetti Integration}}.
{{c1::Enterprise Integration}} is a {{c2::strategic approach}} achieving {{c3::interoperability between IT applications}} that is {{c4::business-driven}} rather than technology-driven.
The business problems solved by integration include {{c1::Information Aggregation}}, providing a {{c2::single point of entry}}, reducing {{c3::process inefficiency}}, and enabling {{c4::web channel & supplier integration}}.
The main Systems Integration Methods are: {{c1::Horizontal Integration}} (uses a subsystem as an interface between all others, also known as an {{c2::Enterprise Service Bus (ESB)}}), {{c3::Vertical Integration}} (creates {{c4::depositories or silos}}), {{c5::Star Integration}} (connects sub-systems using {{c6::point-to-point connections}}), and {{c7::Common data format}}.
The primary types of integration are: {{c1::Enterprise Application Integration (EAI)}} (connects {{c2::internal workflows and applications}} for {{c3::real-time actions}}), {{c4::Data Integration (DI)}} (consolidates {{c5::raw data from multiple databases}} into a {{c6::single repository for analytics}}), and {{c7::Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)}} (facilitates {{c8::secure, computer-to-computer exchange of standardized documents}} between {{c9::external partners}}).
The five general steps in Systems Integration are {{c1::Requirements Gathering}}, {{c2::Data Analysis}}, {{c3::Design Architecture}}, {{c4::Design Systems integration}}, and {{c5::System Implementation}}.
