# Area, Bluetooth, Cellular, and Satellite Networks

**Date:** 2026-10-29

| Cue / Keywords | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Network Categories** | Networks may be categorized by coverage area, connection type, or architecture. This lesson focuses on PAN, LAN, MAN, and WAN by area. |
| **PAN** | Short-range network among personal devices; Bluetooth is a common example. The slide's approximate 10 m figure is an illustration, not a universal boundary. |
| **LAN / WLAN** | LAN connects devices within a home, office, school, or site; Ethernet is common. WLAN connects them wirelessly, often using Wi-Fi and an access point. Wired connections can be stable and high capacity but need cable; wireless offers mobility but is affected by interference and access security. |
| **MAN** | Connects networks over a city or nearby urban area, between LAN and WAN scales. Slide ranges such as 5-50 km are examples, not standards. |
| **WAN** | Connects separate networks over broad geographic distances through long-distance or carrier infrastructure. The Internet is a public example. |
| **Bluetooth** | Short-range radio technology for cable-free device communication. Actual throughput and range vary by version, device, and conditions; slide rates are examples rather than universal limits. |
| **Piconet** | In the classical Bluetooth model, one primary coordinates up to seven active secondary devices. The model is not a universal rule for every Bluetooth version or BLE arrangement. |
| **Scatternet** | Links piconets through a device participating in multiple piconets; a bridge may be secondary in one and primary in another. |
| **Cellular Network** | Divides service coverage into cells; each uses a base station/cell site connected to the broader network. Supports mobile voice, messaging, internet, and connected devices. Actual coverage varies. |
| **Satellite Network** | Uses orbiting relay equipment. Space segment contains satellites and payload; ground segment includes stations, teleports, and network operation centers. Ground stations link the satellite system to other networks. |
| **GEO / MEO / LEO** | GEO is about 36,000 km above the equator, appears stationary, and covers a large area with higher latency. MEO lies between GEO and LEO. LEO is much closer, at several hundred km in the lesson example. |
| **Cellular vs Satellite** | Satellite can extend service to remote locations; cost, delay, weather, and coverage depend on orbit and service. The slide comparison is contextual, not a guarantee of uninterrupted availability. |

---

### Summary
PAN, LAN, MAN, and WAN describe progressively broader network scopes. Bluetooth organizes short-range links; cellular networks serve devices through cells, while satellite systems relay traffic through space and ground infrastructure. Range, speed, and reliability depend on implementation and conditions.
