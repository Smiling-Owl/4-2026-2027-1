# Digital vs. Analog Signals: Course Notes

## I. Fundamental Definitions
Digital and analog represent two different methods used to represent, store, and transmit information[cite: 2].

*   **Analog Systems:** These systems utilize continuous, smooth physical quantities—such as waves or voltages—to mirror real-world changes in elements like sound, light, or temperature[cite: 2].
*   **Digital Systems:** These systems convert information into distinct, discontinuous steps or numbers[cite: 2]. 
*   They typically achieve this using binary code, consisting of 0s and 1s[cite: 2].

## II. Signal Types and Transmission
Understanding the difference between the signals themselves and how they are transmitted is a core concept in data communications.

### A. Digital vs. Analog Signals
*   **Digital Signals:** These are sequences of discrete values that take on one of a finite set of possible values at any given time[cite: 2]. 
*   They are heavily used in modern electronics, including telecommunications, computers, and digital media[cite: 2].
*   **Analog Signals:** These are continuous, time-varying signals that can take on an infinite number of values within a specified range[cite: 2]. 
*   They are primarily used in traditional technologies like radio broadcasting and older television systems[cite: 2].

### B. Digital vs. Analog Transmission
*   **Digital Transmission:** This is the process of sending information in a discrete or binary form using bits (composing 0s and 1s)[cite: 2]. 
*   Its primary advantages include High Accuracy, Efficiency, Multiplexing, Compression, and Security[cite: 2].
*   **Analog Transmission:** This involves sending information in a continuous wave form that is analogous to the original data[cite: 2]. 
*   Its main advantages are Simplicity, Signal Fidelity, and Real-Time Processing[cite: 2].

## III. Signal Conversion
Because the real world is analog and computers are digital, we require converters to bridge the gap.

*   **Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC):** This device allows digital circuits to interface with the real world by encoding an analogue signal into a binary code[cite: 2]. 
*   It enables analog signals originating from sensors or audio inputs to be processed by digital systems like computers or microcontrollers[cite: 2].
*   **Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC):** This device converts a digital input signal into an analog output signal[cite: 2]. 
*   It functions by translating digital values into continuous voltage levels, which recreates the original analog signal[cite: 2].

## IV. Modulation Techniques
Modulation is the technique of adding information to an optical or electrical carrier signal in order to transform data into radio waves[cite: 2]. A carrier signal maintains a consistent frequency and height (amplitude) on its waveform[cite: 2].

Modulation is divided into two primary branches: Analog Modulation and Digital Modulation[cite: 2].

### 1. Analog Modulation 
*   **Amplitude Modulation:** The amplitude of the carrier signal is varied in proportion to the analog message signal[cite: 2].
*   **Frequency Modulation:** The frequency of the carrier signal is varied in proportion to the analog message signal[cite: 2].
*   **Phase Modulation:** The phase of the carrier signal is varied in proportion to the analog message signal[cite: 2].

### 2. Digital Modulation (Shift Keying)
*   **Amplitude-Shift Keying (ASK):** The amplitude of the carrier signal is varied in accordance with the digital data[cite: 2].
*   **Frequency-Shift Keying (FSK):** The frequency of the carrier signal is varied to represent digital data[cite: 2].
*   **Phase-Shift Keying (PSK):** The phase of the carrier signal is shifted to represent digital data[cite: 2].
