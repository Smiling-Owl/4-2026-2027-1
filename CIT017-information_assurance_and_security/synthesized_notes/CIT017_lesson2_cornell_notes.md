# Information Assurance and Security: Social Engineering (Cornell Notes)

## 1. Introduction to Social Engineering

**Cues / Questions**
- What is Social Engineering?
- What is the primary target of Social Engineering?

**Notes**
- **Definition**: The psychological manipulation of individuals into performing actions or divulging confidential information.
- **Target**: Exploits the "human element" (human error, trust, cognitive biases) rather than technical vulnerabilities in software/hardware.

---

## 2. Social Engineering Techniques

**Cues / Questions**
- What are common Email-based attacks?
- How does BEC differ from standard Phishing?
- What are Physical and Network-based attacks?
- What is a Watering Hole Attack?

**Notes**
- **Email-Based**:
  - *Phishing*: Fraudulent mass emails.
  - *Spear Phishing*: Targeted phishing with personalized info.
  - *Whaling*: Targeting high-level executives (CEOs).
  - *Business Email Compromise (BEC)*: Spoofing executives to trigger finance transfers (e.g., $37M Toyota breach).
  - *Clone Phishing*: Intercepting and altering a legitimate email.
- **Phone / Mobile**:
  - *Vishing*: Voice phishing (phone calls).
  - *Deepfake*: AI voice cloning.
  - *Smishing*: SMS/text-based phishing.
- **Physical & Network**:
  - *Tailgating*: Following an authorized person through a secure door.
  - *Dumpster Diving*: Searching trash for sensitive docs.
  - *Shoulder Surfing*: Observing passwords/PINs over someone's shoulder.
  - *Baiting*: Enticing victims with a physical item (e.g., infected USB drive).
  - *Evil Twin*: Fake Wi-Fi access point.
- **Web & Interactive**:
  - *Watering Hole*: Compromising a site the target frequently visits.
  - *Honeytrap*: Romance scam / pig butchering.
  - *Quid Pro Quo*: Service in exchange for info (e.g., fake tech support).
  - *Reverse SE*: Creating a problem so the victim asks the attacker for help.
  - *Pharming/Typosquatting*: Redirecting to fake domains.
- **Social Media**:
  - *Angler Phishing*: Posing as customer support on social media to intercept complaints.

## 3. Psychological Tactics & Principles

**Cues / Questions**
- Why do people fall for these attacks?
- What are Cialdini's principles applied to SE?
- How is curiosity weaponized?

**Notes**
- **System 1 Thinking**: Attackers bypass critical analysis by triggering fast, emotional responses.
- **Authority**: Complying with perceived power (e.g., "I'm from IT").
- **Scarcity / Urgency**: Rushing the victim ("Account suspended in 30 mins").
- **Fear**: Anxiety over consequences ("You've been hacked").
- **Curiosity**: Wanting to know a secret ("Salary Adjustments.xlsx").
- **Greed**: Promising financial gain ("Claim your reward").
- **Liking / Familiarity**: Building rapport or masquerading as a friend.
- **Sympathy**: Appealing to compassion (e.g., fake emergency).
- **Reciprocity**: Offering a free gift/favor to create a sense of debt.
- **Commitment**: Securing a small "yes" before a larger compromise.

## 4. Academic Foundations & Countermeasures

**Cues / Questions**
- What is the "Weakest Link" concept?
- What is SETA?
- How does SE map to the PPT Triad?

**Notes**
- **"Weakest Link"**: The human element is the most vulnerable point in security (Whitman & Rhodes-Ousley).
- **SETA (Security Education, Training, and Awareness)**: The primary organizational defense. Training personnel to recognize manipulation.
- **PPT Triad (People, Process, Technology)**: Attackers exploit People and Process when Technology is strong.
- **Physical Security Integration**: Pretexts can bypass physical access controls (like receptionists) just like digital ones.

---

## Summary

Social Engineering circumvents technical security by exploiting the "human element" through psychological manipulation. Attackers leverage cognitive biases—such as authority, urgency, curiosity, and reciprocity—to deploy attacks across various mediums, including email (Phishing, BEC), phone (Vishing), physical space (Tailgating), and the web (Watering Hole). Because humans are the "weakest link" in the People, Process, and Technology (PPT) triad, organizations must rely on comprehensive SETA (Security Education, Training, and Awareness) programs as their primary countermeasure against these threats.
