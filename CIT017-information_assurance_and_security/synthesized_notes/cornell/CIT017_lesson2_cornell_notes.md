# Information Assurance and Security: Social Engineering (Cornell Notes)

| Cue / Keywords | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Introduction to Social Engineering**<br><br>- What is Social Engineering?<br>- What is the primary target of Social Engineering? | - **Definition**: The psychological manipulation of individuals into performing actions or divulging confidential information.<br>- **Target**: Exploits the "human element" (human error, trust, cognitive biases) rather than technical vulnerabilities in software/hardware. |
| **2. Social Engineering Techniques**<br><br>- What are common Email-based attacks?<br>- How does BEC differ from standard Phishing?<br>- What are Physical and Network-based attacks?<br>- What is a Watering Hole Attack? | - **Email-Based**:<br>- *Phishing*: Fraudulent mass emails.<br>- *Spear Phishing*: Targeted phishing with personalized info.<br>- *Whaling*: Targeting high-level executives (CEOs).<br>- *Business Email Compromise (BEC)*: Spoofing executives to trigger finance transfers (e.g., $37M Toyota breach).<br>- *Clone Phishing*: Intercepting and altering a legitimate email.<br>- **Phone / Mobile**:<br>- *Vishing*: Voice phishing (phone calls).<br>- *Deepfake*: AI voice cloning.<br>- *Smishing*: SMS/text-based phishing.<br>- **Physical & Network**:<br>- *Tailgating*: Following an authorized person through a secure door.<br>- *Dumpster Diving*: Searching trash for sensitive docs.<br>- *Shoulder Surfing*: Observing passwords/PINs over someone's shoulder.<br>- *Baiting*: Enticing victims with a physical item (e.g., infected USB drive).<br>- *Evil Twin*: Fake Wi-Fi access point.<br>- **Web & Interactive**:<br>- *Watering Hole*: Compromising a site the target frequently visits.<br>- *Honeytrap*: Romance scam / pig butchering.<br>- *Quid Pro Quo*: Service in exchange for info (e.g., fake tech support).<br>- *Reverse SE*: Creating a problem so the victim asks the attacker for help.<br>- *Pharming/Typosquatting*: Redirecting to fake domains.<br>- **Social Media**:<br>- *Angler Phishing*: Posing as customer support on social media to intercept complaints. |
| **3. Psychological Tactics & Principles**<br><br>- Why do people fall for these attacks?<br>- What are Cialdini's principles applied to SE?<br>- How is curiosity weaponized? | - **System 1 Thinking**: Attackers bypass critical analysis by triggering fast, emotional responses.<br>- **Authority**: Complying with perceived power (e.g., "I'm from IT").<br>- **Scarcity / Urgency**: Rushing the victim ("Account suspended in 30 mins").<br>- **Fear**: Anxiety over consequences ("You've been hacked").<br>- **Curiosity**: Wanting to know a secret ("Salary Adjustments.xlsx").<br>- **Greed**: Promising financial gain ("Claim your reward").<br>- **Liking / Familiarity**: Building rapport or masquerading as a friend.<br>- **Sympathy**: Appealing to compassion (e.g., fake emergency).<br>- **Reciprocity**: Offering a free gift/favor to create a sense of debt.<br>- **Commitment**: Securing a small "yes" before a larger compromise. |
| **4. Academic Foundations & Countermeasures**<br><br>- What is the "Weakest Link" concept?<br>- What is SETA?<br>- How does SE map to the PPT Triad? | - **"Weakest Link"**: The human element is the most vulnerable point in security (Whitman & Rhodes-Ousley).<br>- **SETA (Security Education, Training, and Awareness)**: The primary organizational defense. Training personnel to recognize manipulation.<br>- **PPT Triad (People, Process, Technology)**: Attackers exploit People and Process when Technology is strong.<br>- **Physical Security Integration**: Pretexts can bypass physical access controls (like receptionists) just like digital ones. |
